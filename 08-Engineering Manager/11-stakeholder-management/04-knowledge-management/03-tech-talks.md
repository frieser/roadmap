## Summary
Tech Talks are formal or semi-formal presentations given by engineering team members to share knowledge, showcase new technologies, or deep-dive into architectural decisions. They are a critical tool for building a learning culture and breaking down knowledge silos.

## Detailed Explanation

### Format
*   **Duration**: Typically 30-45 minutes + Q&A.
*   **Audience**: The wider engineering department.
*   **Topics**: "How we migrated to Go", "Post-mortem of the outage", "Intro to Kafka".

### Benefits
1.  **Knowledge Sharing**: Spreads expertise from one team (e.g., Platform) to others (Product).
2.  **Public Speaking**: Helps engineers build soft skills and confidence.
3.  **Visibility**: Gives junior/senior engineers a platform to be recognized as experts.

### Manager's Role
*   **Sponsor**: Encourage quiet engineers to speak.
*   **Curate**: Ensure diverse topics.
*   **Time**: Explicitly allocate time for preparation (it counts as work).

## Go-Specific Context/Examples

A "Go Meetup" style internal talk.
*   *Topic*: "Common Pitfalls with Go Channels".
*   *Demo*: Show a deadlock, explain why, show the fix.

## Interview Questions

**Q: How do you encourage participation if no one wants to give a Tech Talk?**
**A:** Start small. ask for "Lightning Talks" (5 mins). Lead by example (give one yourself). Offer incentives (swag, lunch). Directly ask an engineer who recently solved a cool problem: "That fix was great, can you share how you found it for 10 mins?"

**Q: What is the difference between a Tech Talk and a Demo?**
**A:** A **Demo** (Sprint Review) focuses on *what* was built (Features/UI) for stakeholders. A **Tech Talk** focuses on *how* it was built (Architecture/Code) for other engineers.

**Q: How do you ensure the talks provide value?**
**A:** Record them for async viewing. Ask for feedback. Ensure the level of technical depth matches the audience (don't explain "What is an API" to seniors).
