## Summary
Brown Bag Sessions are informal training or knowledge-sharing meetings that typically occur during lunch hours (hence the name "brown bag lunch"). They are less structured than Tech Talks and emphasize casual learning and community building.

## Detailed Explanation

### Characteristics
*   **Informal**: Low pressure. Slides are optional. Code walkthroughs are common.
*   **Timing**: Often over lunch (provide food if budget allows!).
*   **Scope**: Can be non-technical (e.g., "Personal Finance for Devs", "Hobbies").

### Goal
To foster a culture of curiosity and psychological safety. Since it's informal, people feel more comfortable asking "stupid questions" than in a formal All-Hands meeting.

### Organization
*   **frequency**: Bi-weekly or Monthly.
*   **Open Floor**: "Anyone learned anything cool this week?"

## Go-Specific Context/Examples

"Go Code Katas".
*   Bring a laptop, project a Go problem (e.g., Advent of Code).
*   Solve it together as a group while eating pizza.
*   Discuss different approaches (Iterative vs Recursive).

## Interview Questions

**Q: Why might "Lunch and Learn" be controversial?**
**A:** Using lunch time can be seen as encroaching on personal break time. It's better to treat it as working hours (provide lunch, or schedule it *near* lunch but allow people to eat). **Never make attendance mandatory** during personal time.

**Q: How do you measure the success of a Brown Bag program?**
**A:** Attendance and Engagement. Are people asking questions? Are different people volunteering to host? It's not about ROI metrics, but about Team Health/Culture.

**Q: Difference from Tech Talk?**
**A:** Tech Talk = Prepared presentation, polished, authoritative. Brown Bag = Discussion, raw, explorative.
